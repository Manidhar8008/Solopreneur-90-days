# Market Realities

This file distinguishes **what we know** from **what we believe**.

## Evidence-backed realities

### 1. Business survival is difficult

BLS reports that 34.7% of U.S. business establishments born in 2013 were still operating in 2023. This is not a solopreneur-specific statistic. Use it to reject complacency, not to predict your individual outcome.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/business-survival-after-10-years.htm

### 2. Cash flow matters independently of accounting profit

The U.S. Small Business Administration emphasizes that cash-flow management is critical and that profitable businesses can still experience cash problems.

Source: https://www.sba.gov/event/85811

### 3. Startup postmortems repeatedly expose market and financial problems

CB Insights' 2026 postmortem analysis of 431 VC-backed companies identified poor product-market fit, bad timing, unsustainable unit economics, and capital exhaustion among recurring failure patterns.

Source: https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/startup-failure-reasons-top/

## Practical market truths

- People buy outcomes, not your learning journey.
- Distribution is part of the product.
- Customer access is an asset.
- A narrow offer is easier to explain than a broad capability.
- Speed of learning matters more than speed of coding.
- Revenue without margin can still produce a bad business.
- Profit without time can still produce a bad life.
- A business that depends on one person is fragile.
- A business that depends on one customer is fragile.
- A business that depends on one platform is fragile.
- A business with no cash buffer is fragile.
- Fancy technology can increase cost before it increases value.
- The easiest customer to reach is often more valuable than the theoretically perfect customer you cannot reach.
